Limassol is the longest town-to-town line running south from Güzelyurt: about 105 kilometres from the plain to the south coast, with one border crossing in between. Some people go for the port and the shops, some for a business meeting or an official appointment, some just to see the sea. A **Güzelyurt Limassol taxi** works the same way for all of them: you are picked up at your door, you cross the line in the same car, and you are dropped wherever you need to be in Limassol. Below is how the run works, how long it takes and what to arrange if you want to come back the same day.

## How long does it take?

From central Güzelyurt to central Limassol is about **105 km** and about **100 minutes**. A queue at the checkpoint can add a little; the rest is the plain road and the southern motorway.

Both directions run on the same line:

- [Güzelyurt – Limassol](/en/routes/guzelyurt-to-limassol/) — heading south
- [Limassol – Güzelyurt](/en/routes/limassol-to-guzelyurt/) — coming home

If you are picked up in Lefke, Gemikonağı or one of the villages, add a few minutes. If you are going somewhere other than the centre — the old port, the marina or one of the seafront hotels — we work out the timing for your address when you book.

## This is not an airport run

On a Larnaca or Paphos trip, the flight sets the clock. In Limassol, you do. There is no flight tracking, no check-in margin and no name board in an arrivals hall; the driver collects you at your door and drops you where you say. That makes it simpler to plan: what matters is when you need to be in Limassol and how you are getting back.

## The border: Bostancı crossing

The shortest way south from Güzelyurt is the **Bostancı (Astromeritis)** crossing at the eastern end of the plain. The Nicosia crossings mean driving east and then doubling back; Bostancı joins the road south directly. The Limassol run always goes this way.

### What happens at the crossing?

There are two checks a few hundred metres apart: one leaving the north, one entering the south. You stay in the car for both. You hand over your passport, the officer looks at it, and you drive on. Passengers pay nothing; the car's papers and insurance are the driver's job. More on the crossing itself in the [Bostancı crossing guide](/en/blog/guzelyurt-bostanci-crossing-guide/).

### What should you carry?

**Your passport, both ways.** You pass the same checks going and coming back, and everyone in the car needs one. Visa and entry rules can depend on nationality; the details are in the [North Cyprus passport and border crossing guide](/en/blog/north-cyprus-visa-passport-border-crossing/).

## Why a taxi and not a hire car?

This is where people get caught out on this line. Cars hired in the north often cannot be taken south at all; even when they can, the rental insurance usually does not cover the south, and you have to buy separate cover at the crossing. Then there is parking in Limassol.

With a taxi, none of that is your problem: one car, both sides, no paperwork on yours. The options are compared side by side in [private transfer vs taxi vs car hire](/en/blog/north-cyprus-transfer-vs-taxi-vs-car-hire/).

## Coming back the same day

Most Limassol trips are day trips: down in the morning, back in the evening. There are two ways to do it:

- **The car waits.** The driver waits for you in Limassol and brings you back when you are done. If your appointment or errand takes a few hours, this is the easiest option.
- **A separate return pickup.** You are dropped off and collected again at an agreed time and place. If your day is long, or you are not sure how long you will stay, this fits better.

Say which you want when you book; waiting and the return are agreed before you set off, too. Confirm the return place and time before you are dropped off. If your plans run late, just let us know and the time moves with you. If the return ends up late in the evening, the [Güzelyurt night taxi guide](/en/blog/guzelyurt-night-taxi/) helps.

## Two sides, two currencies

The north uses Turkish lira, the south uses euros. Cards work in most places in Limassol, but a little cash for each side makes the day easier. Practical notes on money and tipping are in the [North Cyprus money and tipping guide](/en/blog/north-cyprus-money-tipping-guide/).

## How is the fare set?

The fare is **per car, not per person**. It is given before you set off and then fixed: no meter, and it does not change with traffic or a queue at the border. You get the figure from Denizli Taksi when you book or over the phone. If you want the car to wait or a return pickup, ask at the same time; all of it is agreed up front.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a taxi from Güzelyurt to Limassol take?

About 100 minutes for 105 km. A queue at the Bostancı checkpoint can add a little.

### Can I cross the border by taxi from Güzelyurt to Limassol?

Yes. The car crosses with you, so there is no change of vehicle in the south. You stay in the car at the checkpoint and show your passport.

### What do I need for the crossing?

A passport for everyone in the car, in both directions. Rules can depend on nationality, so check your own situation before you travel.

### Can I drive a car hired in the north to Limassol?

Usually not. Cars hired in the north often cannot be taken south, and rental insurance rarely covers the south. A taxi removes the problem.

### Can I come back the same day?

Yes. The car can wait for you in Limassol, or collect you again at a time you agree. Say which you want when you book.

### How is the Güzelyurt Limassol taxi fare calculated?

The fare is per car, fixed before you set off, and there is no meter. You get the figure when you book or over the phone; waiting and the return are agreed up front too.

### Can I get a taxi back from Limassol to Güzelyurt?

Yes, arranged in advance. Agree the return place and time before you are dropped off, and Denizli Taksi will be there.
