<p>Calling a <strong>Lefke taxi</strong> is not like catching one in Kyrenia or Nicosia. Lefke sits at the western end of the island — not a place traffic passes through, but the place the road runs out. Nobody drives through here looking for a fare. The town, the campus, the Gemikonağı seafront and the villages up the coast all run off one booked line, and the car comes because you called it.</p>

<h2>Why cars here are booked, not flagged down</h2>
<p>Stand on a square in Kyrenia and a taxi will eventually pass. In Lefke it will not. A few cars move around town during the day, and after late afternoon there is effectively nothing. That is geography, not neglect: the road into Lefke ends at Lefke.</p>
<p>So the arrangement is simple. You say where you are and where you are going, the fare is agreed before the car sets off, and it comes to the address rather than to a meeting point — the campus gate, the seafront, or a village along the coast road. No meter runs; the figure is settled first.</p>

<h2>European University of Lefke and campus runs</h2>
<p>European University of Lefke sits in the town itself, and the health sciences campus and METU Northern Cyprus at Kalkanlı are along the same road east — three campuses on one stretch. That is this line's daily work: dorm-to-campus trips, late lectures, exam mornings, and students arriving with a term's luggage.</p>
<p>When you call, name the faculty or the dorm block rather than saying "the campus". There is more than one entrance, and the right gate is the difference between waiting on the wrong side in the dark and having the car pull up in front of you. For the campus lines in full, see the <a href="/en/blog/metu-ncc-lefke-campus-transfer-guide/">METU and Lefke student taxi guide</a>.</p>

<h2>Gemikonağı, the coast and the western villages</h2>
<p>Lefke is more than the town. The Gemikonağı seafront, the Soli mosaics and Vouni Palace, Yeşilırmak at the western tip, and the villages between them — Denizli, Gaziveren, Yedidalga, Bademliköy, Cengizköy, Çamlıköy, Doğancı, Yeşilyurt — are all on the same number. No village has a rank of its own, and none needs one.</p>
<p>In a village the fastest way to describe where you are is a landmark rather than a street: the mosque, the school, the muhtar's office, the shop or the football pitch. The village name plus that point is enough. There is more on this in the <a href="/en/blog/guzelyurt-lefke-village-taxi/">Güzelyurt and Lefke village taxi guide</a>.</p>

<h2>Airport runs: Ercan, Larnaca and Paphos</h2>
<p>Lefke connects to all three airports, but the distances are far from equal.</p>
<h3>Ercan Airport</h3>
<p>About eighty kilometres east and seventy minutes. The drive runs through Güzelyurt with no border crossing on the way. Book with your flight number and the landing time is tracked, so a delay does not cost you the car. The line: <a href="/en/routes/lefke-to-ercan-airport/">Lefke to Ercan Airport</a>, and the return direction, <a href="/en/routes/ercan-airport-to-lefke/">Ercan Airport to Lefke</a>.</p>
<h3>Larnaca and Paphos</h3>
<p>Both are reached through the <strong>Bostancı (Astromeritis)</strong> crossing rather than the Nicosia gates — the quick way out of this side of the island. Paphos is the shortest southern run from Lefke, straight down the west coast. The lines: <a href="/en/routes/lefke-to-larnaca-airport/">Lefke to Larnaca Airport</a> and <a href="/en/routes/lefke-to-paphos-airport/">Lefke to Paphos Airport</a>.</p>

<h2>The Yeşilırmak (Limnitis) crossing</h2>
<p>Yeşilırmak is the gate at the western tip and the closest crossing to Lefke — noticeably nearer than Bostancı. Two things to plan around: it opens in daylight hours only, and it takes cars rather than foot traffic. For a late return, checking the hours on the phone beats arriving at a closed gate.</p>

<h2>What to say when you call</h2>
<p>Three things shorten the conversation:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Where you are</strong> — the village or neighbourhood, plus a landmark. If there is nothing to name, send your location on WhatsApp.</li>
  <li><strong>How many of you and how many cases</strong> — especially at the start of term and on airport runs, so the right car comes.</li>
  <li><strong>The time</strong> — for an early flight or a late lecture, calling ahead makes the car certain. There is no spare car idling in the evening.</li>
</ul>
<p>Once those three are clear the fare is agreed and fixed before the car sets off. The line is run on the ground by <strong>Denizli Taksi</strong>; the full picture is on the <a href="/en/areas/lefke-taxi/">Lefke Taxi</a> page, and the neighbouring line on <a href="/en/areas/guzelyurt-taxi/">Guzelyurt Taxi</a>.</p>

<p>Yes, by arrangement rather than by walking out and finding one. Late finishes, pre-dawn departures and the evening run home are standard here — the earlier the call, the surer the car.</p>
