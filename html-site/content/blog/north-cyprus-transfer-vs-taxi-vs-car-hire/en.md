<p>For most arrivals, a pre-booked private transfer is the best value: you know the price before you fly, a named chauffeur meets you at arrivals, and there is nothing to arrange once you land. An airport taxi can be cheaper on a short hop but the fare is not fixed; a rental car only pays off if you plan to drive every day; and the kombi is the cheapest of all but does not go door to door. Here is how the four options really compare.</p>

<h2>How much does an airport transfer cost in North Cyprus?</h2>
<p>A private transfer is priced per vehicle, not per person, and it is fixed when you book. As a guide, Ercan Airport to Kyrenia is around £45–£55, and Larnaca to Kyrenia — which crosses the border — is roughly £80–£95. Because tolls, parking and the first 45 minutes of airport waiting are included, the number you see when you book is the number you pay.</p>

<h2>Is an airport taxi cheaper than a transfer?</h2>
<p>Sometimes, on a very short trip — but the fare is not fixed. Airport taxis in North Cyprus rarely run a meter, so you agree a price at the rank, often late at night, with luggage, after a flight. A typical Ercan–Kyrenia taxi is quoted at roughly €50–€63, and a night or a busy arrival can push it higher. If you value knowing the cost in advance, that uncertainty is the real difference, not the headline number.</p>

<h2>Should I hire a car instead?</h2>
<p>Rent a car only if you intend to drive most days. Two things surprise first-time visitors. Cyprus drives on the left, so the roads take a day to get used to. And if you land in the south and drive to the north, you must buy separate North Cyprus insurance at the border — a rental booked in the south is not covered across the Green Line by default. For a beach-and-hotel holiday with a few outings, a transfer in and a couple of day trips usually costs less than a week of hire, fuel and parking.</p>

<h2>What about the kombi or public minibus?</h2>
<p>The kombi is the cheapest way to move around and a fine choice for a solo traveller with a small bag. The trade-offs are simple: it runs set routes to set stops, not to your hotel door; it does not wait for a delayed flight; and with cases, late at night, or as a family it stops being practical. It is a great way to explore on a spare afternoon — just not the way most people want to start a holiday at 1 a.m.</p>

<h2>So which should you choose?</h2>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Landing tired, with luggage or family, or crossing the border:</strong> a private transfer — fixed price, met at arrivals, flight tracked.</li>
  <li><strong>One very short hop and you enjoy haggling:</strong> an airport taxi can work, if you agree the price first.</li>
  <li><strong>Touring the island most days:</strong> hire a car, and buy the border insurance if you cross.</li>
  <li><strong>Solo, light, and counting every pound:</strong> the kombi for getting around once you have arrived.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Do I pay per person or per car?</h3>
<p>Per car. The fixed fare covers the whole vehicle up to its seat count, so a family or group shares one price rather than paying each.</p>
<h3>Can a taxi or transfer cross from Larnaca to the north?</h3>
<p>A pre-booked transfer can and does daily — you stay in the same car through the Green Line checkpoint, with no passenger fee. Many south-based taxis do not cross, so book a driver who does.</p>
