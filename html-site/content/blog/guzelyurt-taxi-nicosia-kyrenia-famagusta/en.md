<p>Most trips out of Güzelyurt (Morphou) don't head for an airport — they head for a town: an appointment in Nicosia, dinner in Kyrenia, university in Famagusta. These three lines connect the plain to the rest of the north, and each one calls for a different kind of day. Below is how a <strong>Güzelyurt taxi to Nicosia, Kyrenia and Famagusta</strong> works — how long it takes, what each line is good for, and how a same-day return is arranged.</p>

<h2>Three towns, three lines</h2>
<p>All three lines stay in the north: no crossing point, no passport check, no queue. The fare is per car, agreed and fixed before you set off.</p>

<p>Times are door to door, daytime, in normal traffic. The in-town stretch of Nicosia can run longer at evening rush hour; for trips with an appointment we build that into the departure time from the start.</p>

<h2>Güzelyurt – Nicosia: offices and appointments</h2>
<p>Nicosia is the town people from Güzelyurt travel to most, and usually not for sightseeing but for errands: ministries and government offices, the land registry, consulates, a doctor's appointment, a big shop. The drive is about <strong>45 km and 35 minutes</strong>; leave early and you are in town before offices open.</p>
<p>The question we hear most on this line is about the return. If you don't know how long your appointment will take, there are two options: the car drops you and comes back when you call, or the same car waits at the door. We decide together at booking which one fits your day — waiting is never left as a surprise.</p>
<p>If you want to walk across to the south once you are done in Nicosia, the car can drop you close to the Ledra Street (Lokmacı) crossing. If you are driving south, the western crossing is shorter than going through Nicosia — see the <a href="/en/blog/guzelyurt-bostanci-crossing-guide/">Bostancı crossing guide</a>.</p>

<h2>Güzelyurt – Kyrenia: harbour, dinner and hotels</h2>
<p>The road to Kyrenia doesn't go through Nicosia; it climbs north out of the plain, crosses the western end of the mountains and drops down to the coast. The trip is about <strong>55 km and 45 minutes</strong>. Kyrenia's hotel strip spreads out on both sides of town, so the address matters: a restaurant at the harbour and a hotel in Alsancak or Çatalköy are not the same drop-off — just give us the exact name.</p>
<p>Most people go to Kyrenia in the evening and come back at night. That makes it the Güzelyurt taxi trip that most needs talking through in advance: if your return is close to midnight, we set the car aside for that time from the start. Finding a car in Kyrenia that will drive back to Güzelyurt late at night is hard and expensive; booking the return together with the outbound is the cleanest way.</p>
<p>If you are going to Kyrenia to look around, the <a href="/en/blog/kyrenia-harbour-castle-old-town-guide/">Kyrenia harbour and castle guide</a> makes planning the day easier.</p>

<h2>Güzelyurt – Famagusta: university and a long day</h2>
<p>Famagusta is the longest of the three lines: west of the island to the east, about <strong>105 km and 75 minutes</strong>. Two groups make this trip most often: students at Eastern Mediterranean University, or visiting friends there, and visitors who want to see the walled city, Salamis or the eastern beaches.</p>
<p>For students moving at the start of term, luggage is what matters: tell us how many suitcases at booking and the car is arranged accordingly. For sightseers, a tip: Famagusta alone takes half a day, add Salamis and it becomes a full day. Depending on what you want to see, take a look at the <a href="/en/blog/famagusta-beaches-old-city-guide/">Famagusta beaches and old city guide</a>.</p>

<h2>There and back in a day</h2>
<p>On all three lines the most requested pattern is the same: go in the morning, get things done, come back in the evening. There are two ways to set it up:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Two separate trips:</strong> the car drops you off and picks you up from the same place at the time you set. Best if you are spending the whole day in town.</li>
  <li><strong>A waiting car:</strong> if your business takes an hour or two, the car stays and brings you back. Common for appointments in Nicosia.</li>
</ul>
<p>Either way, the fare is agreed before you leave. If you don't know your return time exactly, give an estimate; if you finish early, just call.</p>

<h2>The other way: from town to Güzelyurt</h2>
<p>The same lines run in reverse. If you need to get back to Güzelyurt from a flight, a hotel or a friend's place in town, give us the address and the car picks you up there: <a href="/en/routes/nicosia-to-guzelyurt/">Nicosia – Güzelyurt</a>, <a href="/en/routes/kyrenia-to-guzelyurt/">Kyrenia – Güzelyurt</a> and <a href="/en/routes/famagusta-to-guzelyurt/">Famagusta – Güzelyurt</a>. If you are heading back to one of Güzelyurt's villages or to Lefke, give the final stop by village name — the <a href="/en/blog/guzelyurt-lefke-village-taxi/">villages guide</a> lists the villages we cover.</p>

<h2>How is the fare set?</h2>
<p>We don't publish a fixed tariff for these lines; the fare is quoted per trip by phone or at booking. The quoted figure is fixed before you set off: no meter, and it doesn't change with traffic or a longer road. The fare is per car, not per person — if four of you share the car, you split it four ways. You pay the driver in cash on arrival; no prepayment or deposit is taken. How booking works step by step is in <a href="/en/blog/guzelyurt-taxi-how-to-book/">how to book a Güzelyurt taxi</a>.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>How long is a taxi from Güzelyurt to Nicosia?</h3>
<p>About 45 km and 35 minutes, door to door. The in-town stretch can run longer at Nicosia's evening rush hour; for trips with an appointment we set the departure time accordingly.</p>
<h3>How long is a taxi from Güzelyurt to Kyrenia?</h3>
<p>About 55 km and 45 minutes. The road heads north without entering Nicosia and crosses the western end of the mountains down to the coast.</p>
<h3>Is there a taxi from Güzelyurt to Famagusta?</h3>
<p>Yes. It is a trip of about 105 km and 75 minutes, entirely in the north with no crossing. For students travelling with luggage, give the number of suitcases at booking.</p>
<h3>Can I go and come back the same day?</h3>
<p>Yes. The car can drop you and pick you up at the time you set, or wait at the door if your business is short. Which one, and the fare, are agreed before you set off.</p>
<h3>Can I get back from Kyrenia to Güzelyurt late at night?</h3>
<p>Yes, but give the return time in advance. For returns close to midnight the car is set aside from the start; booking the return with the outbound is much easier than looking for a car in Kyrenia at night.</p>
<h3>Is the fare per person?</h3>
<p>No, it is per car. The fare is quoted by phone or at booking, fixed before you set off, and paid to the driver in cash on arrival.</p>
